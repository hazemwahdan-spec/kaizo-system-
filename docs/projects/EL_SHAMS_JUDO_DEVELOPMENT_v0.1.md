## 1. Executive mandate

Build an integrated, age-appropriate, evidence-led judo development system that converts the club's existing facilities, athlete base and 34-person judo/fitness/medical staff into sustained competitive performance.

The project covers:
1. Strategic diagnosis and root-cause analysis.
2. Age-group and development-level architecture.
3. Athlete physical, technical and tactical standards.
4. Valid and reliable assessment battery.
5. Annual-to-session training planning.
6. Facility, mat, gym and equipment utilization.
7. Staff roles, coach development and evaluation.
8. Competition preparation and performance analysis.
9. Athlete welfare, medical monitoring and injury prevention.
10. KPIs, monthly reviews and KAIZO improvement cycles.

## 3. Strategic diagnosis — initial hypotheses, not final findings

| Area | Known input | Hypothesis / risk to test | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Athlete grouping | Large mixed groups reported | Group size and mixed readiness may reduce individual coaching and task suitability | Rosters, mat capacity, coach-to-athlete ratio, session observations |
| Athlete pathway | Beginners and experienced athletes reportedly mixed | Content and load may not match training age or readiness | Athlete profiles, attendance, training age, skill assessment |
| Coaching | 34-person combined staff; role split unknown | Role clarity, technical consistency and planning quality may vary | Staff register, qualifications, observation rubric, plans |
| Measurement | No common standards reported | Selection and progression decisions may be subjective | Existing records, test audit, pilot battery |
| Training design | Planning gaps reported | Session content may not link consistently to annual goals and competition demands | Annual plans, microcycles, session plans, load records |
| Physical preparation | Dedicated gym and equipment available | Gym work may not be individualized or connected to judo needs | Gym timetable, programs, supervision and safety audit |
| Competition | Results supplied above | Participation volume may not convert efficiently into podium and team points | Draws, brackets, match video, score sheets, weight-by-weight points |

### Preliminary SWOT
- **Strengths:** dedicated facilities; six-mat team hall; separate school hall; dedicated gym; sizable staff group; established athlete pipeline; historical competitive success.
- **Weaknesses to validate:** absence of unified standards and measurement; mixed readiness groups; unclear staff accountability; insufficient documented periodization and performance feedback.
- **Opportunities:** school-to-team talent pathway; mat-station organization; age-appropriate testing; coach mentoring; match-video analysis; integrated physical preparation.
- **Threats to monitor:** athlete overload or injury; attrition caused by poor grouping; growth/maturation differences; competition-rule changes; loss of coaching continuity; pressure to prioritize short-term medals over long-term development.

- **KAIZO project support:** maintain SSOT project records, decision log, evidence register, KPI definitions, versioning and review cadence.
- **Coaches:** execute approved plans, record attendance and observations, report readiness and risks.

## 5. Athlete development architecture

Athletes should be grouped using two independent dimensions:
1. **Competition age / federation eligibility:** preserved exactly according to current governing-body rules.
2. **Development readiness:** foundation, developing, advanced, performance pathway.

Readiness classification uses training age, movement quality, technical execution, tactical behavior, physical development, attendance and learning rate. It is not a permanent label and must be reviewed periodically.

Do not mix athletes solely because they share an age, and do not move athletes across official age/weight categories to solve scheduling problems. Use differentiated tasks and stations where full separation is not feasible.

### Proposed six-mat operating concept (pilot only)
- Mat allocation is decided after measuring usable area, mat safety zones, session overlap and coach availability.
- Each mat/station has one defined learning objective, assigned coach and athlete cap based on safe supervision and drill type.
- Separate technical-learning tasks from high-intensity randori where necessary.
- Do not assume that six mats automatically means six groups; the final number depends on floor plan, age, contact intensity and staffing.

### School-to-team pathway
School hall introduces safe movement, ukemi, coordination, basic judo literacy and enjoyment. Transfer to team pathway follows transparent readiness criteria, coach review and guardian communication—not medal potential alone.

## 6. Measurement and standards framework

Build an age- and development-stage-specific battery with four domains:

### A. Physical
Movement competency, acceleration/speed, agility/change of direction, balance, coordination, grip-related capacity, age-appropriate strength, jump/throw power where safe, and judo-specific intermittent effort.

### B. Technical
Ukemi, posture and movement, tai-sabaki, kuzushi-tsukuri-kake quality, uchi-komi quality, nage-komi control, ne-waza fundamentals, transitions and safe execution on both sides where appropriate.

### C. Tactical / decision
Kumi-kata behavior, opportunity recognition, attack selection, timing, reaction to opponent movement, defense-to-attack transition, appropriate counter decisions, and randori transfer without excessive coach prompting.

### D. Availability and development
Attendance, training age, growth/maturation context where appropriately and privately assessed, injury/absence record, perceived readiness and athlete wellbeing.

**Testing governance**
- Baseline, periodic, pre-competition (when useful and safe), post-competition review, end-of-season.
- Standardize warm-up, surface, equipment, instructions, assessor, trial count and recovery.
- Pilot protocols before adopting club norms.
- Establish reliability (repeatability), assessor agreement and age appropriateness before setting cut scores.
- Report individual change and uncertainty; avoid false precision.
- Do not apply adult norms or use weight alone as a proxy for ability.
- Medical clearance and safeguarding take precedence over testing targets.

## 7. Training-planning architecture

Create connected plans at:
1. Multi-year athlete-development pathway.
2. Annual plan aligned to the actual competition calendar.
3. Training phase / mesocycle.
4. Weekly microcycle.
5. Daily session.
6. Drill-level execution and coach observation.

Each session should state: age/readiness group, objective, drill, duration, reps/sets, intensity or effort target, rest, partner system, randori transfer, safety notes, coach cues, KPI and post-session review.

Plan physical work in coordination with technical demands, growth, recovery, competition schedule and medical guidance. Do not prescribe a fixed percentage split or load before age-stage data, calendar and current training volumes are known.

## 8. Facilities and equipment utilization

- **Six-mat team hall:** pilot station model with objective, group, coach, cap, safety boundary and rotation.
- **School hall:** introductory curriculum, attendance and talent-development record; formal pathway review.
- **Dedicated gym:** age-appropriate movement and strength competency first; progressive resistance and power only with qualified supervision, suitable technique and individual readiness.
- **Wall bars / parallel bars / climbing rope:** use only for defined movement, coordination, upper-body support or climbing objectives after equipment inspection, age suitability and supervision plan.
- **Medical / fitness staff:** integrate preparticipation screening, injury prevention, return-to-training communication, load/wellbeing monitoring and referral pathways within professional scope.

## 9. Staff development and evaluation

First establish the exact 34-person staff register: name/role, qualifications, licenses, experience, assigned groups, availability, responsibilities and professional-development needs.

Coach evaluation should cover:
- Annual and session planning quality.
- Technical accuracy and safe skill teaching.
- Group management and differentiation.
- Observation and feedback quality.
- Athlete progression and retention.
- Use of data and documentation.
- Competition preparation and post-match learning.
- Professional conduct, safeguarding and collaboration.

Do not evaluate a coach by medal count alone. Use observation, plan review, athlete progression, safety and peer/technical-director review.

## 10. Competition-performance system

For every event, capture:
- Eligible and entered athletes by age/weight.
- Draws, opponents and match outcomes.
- Points scored/conceded and decisive actions.
- Shido / penalties and rule-specific events.
- Match duration and contest context.
- Attack attempts, effective attacks, defensive actions, transitions and tactical decisions.
- Stage reached, placement and official team-points calculation.

Analyze participation-to-performance conversion and points by weight/category. Compare the club with competitors only using verified event records and consistent scoring rules.

## 12. First 30-day action plan

### Days 1–7: Establish the single source of truth
1. Create master roster by birth year, sex, weight, training age, experience, attendance and competition eligibility.
2. Create staff register for all 34 people and identify actual roles and availability.
3. Map hall timetable, mat use, gym timetable and equipment condition.
4. Collect the last three seasons of official competition results, brackets, team-point calculations and match videos where available.
5. Collect existing annual plans, weekly plans, session logs and medical/absence records under appropriate access controls.

### Days 8–14: Observe and map
1. Observe representative sessions across age and readiness groups.
2. Measure actual athlete-to-coach ratios and usable mat capacity.
3. Map athlete pathways from school to team.
4. Audit coach plans and delivery using a common rubric.
5. Identify immediate safety, injury and safeguarding gaps.

### Days 15–21: Pilot assessment and grouping
1. Approve a small, age-appropriate pilot battery.
2. Train assessors and standardize protocols.
3. Pilot movement, technical and tactical rubrics with a representative sample.
4. Draft proposed grouping and station schedules without making irreversible selections.
5. Validate data quality and athlete/guardian communication.

### Days 22–30: Approve the first operating model
1. Present a verified diagnostic report with confidence levels and missing evidence.
2. Approve group architecture and coach assignments.
3. Approve first 90-day training and coach-development priorities.
4. Publish dashboard v0.1 with baseline status and named owners.
5. Record technical-director decisions and review date.

## 13. Days 31–90: Controlled implementation
- Implement the approved grouping model as a reversible pilot.
- Start individualized athlete profiles and periodic monitoring.
- Deploy age-stage session templates and weekly planning review.
- Integrate supervised gym work with mat objectives.
- Observe each coach and agree a development plan.
- Run the first standardized reassessment after an appropriate interval.
- Conduct internal randori/competition simulations and code performance.
- Review athlete welfare, attendance, injuries and retention monthly.
- Revise based on evidence; do not increase training volume automatically.

## 14. Six-to-twelve-month outcomes
- A functioning school-to-team pathway.
- Documented standards and assessment protocols by age/development stage.
- Consistent planning and coach observation.
- Improved physical, technical and tactical development measured against baseline.
- Better conversion from competition entry to progression, podiums and team points.
- Sustainable athlete retention, health and coach capability.
- A season-end review that informs the next annual plan.

The overall shield is a strategic outcome, not a guaranteed result. The project commits to controllable performance processes and transparent evaluation.
